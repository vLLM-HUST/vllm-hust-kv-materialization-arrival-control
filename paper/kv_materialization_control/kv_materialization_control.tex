\begin{abstract}
KV reuse can avoid prompt computation, but retrieving, aligning, and committing
state also costs time. We evaluated an arrival-time controller that selects full
reuse, block-aligned segmented reuse, or recomputation, and instrumented the
serving path to distinguish requested, effective, and engine-realized actions.
This paper reports a boundary, rather than a new controller: in a
preregistered, graph-mode repeated online matrix, the controller improves over
recomputation but loses to the best fixed policy. Mean matched TTFT regresses
by 9.24\% and 6.60\% on the two tested workloads, and
\texttt{always\_full\_reuse} wins all six rounds. A separate paired Ascend
graph/eager diagnostic finds no repeatable graph-only overlap gap. We retain a
block-aligned segmented-tail carrier with realized-action accounting, this
negative online boundary, and a provenance boundary. Of 141 completed
controller lifecycles, 39 have retained per-request raw bundles; 102 are
aggregate-only supporting evidence. Offline cost-model results are model
analysis, never online benefit. This is a candidate artifact, not
submission-ready.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Prefix caching makes reusable KV state available, but availability is not a
latency guarantee. A serving path may retrieve the state, align it to runtime
blocks, commit it into the engine cache, and recompute an unmatched tail.
Existing systems already expose much of this space: prefix caching and cache
management~\cite{vllm2023,lmcache}; I/O-aware partial
recomputation~\cite{kvpr2025,ellm2026}; non-prefix cache fusion and
position-aware correction~\cite{cacheblend2025,cacheslide2026}; and
disaggregated storage and transfer~\cite{distserve2024,mooncake2025}.

This repository originally asked whether a small arrival-time controller could
choose full reuse, partial reuse, or recomputation better than fixed policies.
That is not a positive novelty claim: LMCache can skip a small retrieval for
recomputation~\cite{lmcache}, KVPR jointly plans partial transfer and
recomputation~\cite{kvpr2025}, and eLLM adapts partial-token caching and
recomputation~\cite{ellm2026}. Our knowledge increment is an evidence-bounded
systems result: on this carrier and matched serving setup, the tested controller
does not beat full reuse.

The retained contributions are deliberately narrow:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item A block-aligned segmented-tail carrier and scheduler accounting that
separate requested, effective, and engine-realized actions.
\item A preregistered repeated real-online comparison establishing a negative
benefit boundary for the present controller.
\item A paired graph/eager diagnostic with no independent graph-only overlap
gap, plus a raw-versus-aggregate provenance boundary.
\end{enumerate}
We do not claim a KV store, transfer system, admission/eviction policy,
multi-granularity index, arbitrary token-level materialization, or universal
speedup. A burst-arrival hypothesis remains outside this paper until its
separate gate passes.

\section{Design: Scope and Runtime Boundary}
At request arrival the controller can request full reuse, a segmented prefix,
or recomputation. The carrier supports native full reuse and recomputation. For
partial reuse, it caps lookup and shared-cache commit at an aligned block
boundary and isolates the recomputed tail in a request-scoped hash namespace.
Thus, partial reuse is block-aligned segmented reuse, not arbitrary token-level
materialization.

The runtime records three quantities. Requested is the policy output; effective
is the action after alignment and dominance checks; and realized is the engine
counter. It also records lookup, alignment, cache commit, tail isolation,
recomputed-tail tokens, and fallback reasons. A requested partial action is
therefore never reported as realized reuse.

\section{Evidence Classes and Method}
We label every result by evidence class. Offline cost-model analysis uses
workload-derived inputs to inspect the three-action surface and oracle headroom;
it cannot demonstrate a serving gain. Real-online evidence comes from fresh
service lifecycles through the live path. Derived artifacts are tables and
verdicts rebuilt from those data. A model-selected partial action is never
promoted to realized reuse or online speedup.

The retained online bundles and generated reconstruction are under
\url{experiments/results/m2_online_boundary_20260803/}; the independent
graph/eager traces and custody manifest are under \url{docs/wavemat/}.
These repository sources are the evidence for the carrier, online, and
diagnostic statements below.

The primary online study uses Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, graph mode, block size 128,
two existing workloads, three temporally blocked rounds per workload, and a
fresh lifecycle for every controller/fixed-policy cell. It compares always
recompute, always full reuse, and the current controller. The lifecycle, not an
individual request, is the statistical unit. All 18/18 retained raw bundles
validate, containing 576/576 successful requests. Earlier single-run
descriptive matrices are not used as online performance evidence.

The controller loses to the best fixed policy in both workloads: matched mean
TTFT is $+9.24\%$ for tool scaffold and $+6.60\%$ for knowledge service.
Always full reuse wins all six matched rounds. End-to-end latency
($+0.16\%$, $+0.66\%$) and throughput ($-0.16\%$, $-0.68\%$) do not reverse
that finding. The offline cost model identifies the same winner in 6/6 rounds,
but this validates only the ranking for this experiment, not an online proof.

Catalog closure is aggregate-only supporting evidence: across four promoted
workloads and 20 confirmation rounds, always full reuse again wins every round,
with no preregistered $\geq 5\%$ controller gain. The corresponding raw bundles
are not retained, so this result is not presented as independently
per-request-auditable evidence.

\section{Graph/Eager Diagnostic Boundary}
The separate graph/eager diagnostic tests an upstream layerwise KV path on a
TP=1 Ascend 910B2 with DeepSeek-V2-Lite. It uses paired eager and PIECEWISE
graph arms at static prefetch 1/2/4 and retains eight raw trace assets with
content checksums. Device-timeline overlap is low in both modes (0--4.62\%),
but no repeatable graph-only regression is observed; the p=2 gate trace
correlates all 52 eligible prefetch loads in each mode. Paired replay checks
are also clean. This is a Stop for a graph-specific mechanism, not an
end-to-end acceleration result and not evidence for a new overlap runtime.

\section{Limitations, Provenance, and Conclusion}
Of 141 completed controller lifecycles, 39 have in-repository/release raw bundles and
can be checked request-by-request: 18 main-matrix, 12 pilot, and 9 confirmation
lifecycles. The remaining 102 have per-run aggregates and verdicts only because
their raw bundles were not preserved; they are aggregate-only supporting
evidence. The graph/eager diagnostic separately retains 8/8 raw trace assets with checksums.
Generated tables are derived artifacts, not another evidence class.

The result is bounded to the tested Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct/Ascend carrier,
workload catalog, policy, and repeated protocol. It does not prove an
arrival-time policy cannot help under another candidate source, storage system,
or arrival pattern. It does not turn block alignment into token-level
materialization. Partial reuse remains workload-dependent in the offline model
and did not establish an online benefit here.

The verdict is \texttt{stop\_mechanism\_direction}: the present controller is
closed as a negative result without per-workload retuning or post-hoc workload
search. The burst-arrival question is a separate candidate hypothesis, not a
paper contribution. Until a target venue is selected and checked, this remains
a candidate artifact draft, not a submission-ready manuscript.
