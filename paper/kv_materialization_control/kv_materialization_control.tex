\begin{abstract}
KV reuse can avoid prompt computation, but retrieving, aligning, and committing
state also costs time. We evaluated an arrival-time controller that selects full
reuse, block-aligned segmented reuse, or recomputation, and instrumented the
serving path to distinguish requested, effective, and engine-realized actions.
We report two deliberately separate online protocols. In a five-point,
900-second Qwen3.5-35B-A3B/Ascend observation, the controller-enabled runtime
has a 7.13\% geometric-mean output-throughput gain over the exact published
Native series. The gain is load dependent: $-1.04\%$ at concurrency 1 and
$+9.22\%$, $+11.90\%$, and $+15.16\%$ at concurrency 4, 8, and 16. Controller
and scheduler telemetry show that recompute and reuse paths ran, but all 30
proposed partial actions fell back because available full reuse dominated; the
observation therefore does not establish a partial-materialization benefit. In
a separate preregistered Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct repeated matrix, the controller
loses to the best fixed policy by 9.24\% and 6.60\% mean TTFT, and full reuse
wins all six rounds plus 20 catalog-confirmation rounds. Together, the results
show a positive high-load operating region and a fixed-policy boundary, while
requested, effective, and engine-realized actions remain distinct. We retain
the 39-raw/102-aggregate provenance boundary of the repeated M2 study.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Prefix caching makes reusable KV state available, but availability is not a
latency guarantee. A serving path may retrieve the state, align it to runtime
blocks, commit it into the engine cache, and recompute an unmatched tail.
Existing systems already expose much of this space: prefix caching and cache
management~\cite{vllm2023,lmcache}; I/O-aware partial
recomputation~\cite{kvpr2025,ellm2026}; non-prefix cache fusion and
position-aware correction~\cite{cacheblend2025,cacheslide2026}; and
disaggregated storage and transfer~\cite{distserve2024,mooncake2025}.

This repository originally asked whether a small arrival-time controller could
choose full reuse, partial reuse, or recomputation better than fixed policies.
That is not a positive novelty claim: LMCache can skip a small retrieval for
recomputation~\cite{lmcache}, KVPR jointly plans partial transfer and
recomputation~\cite{kvpr2025}, and eLLM adapts partial-token caching and
recomputation~\cite{ellm2026}. Our knowledge increment is an evidence-bounded
systems result across two non-transferable protocols: the unified Qwen3.5
runtime shows a positive throughput observation that grows with concurrency,
while the matched Qwen2.5 M2 study shows that arrival-time selection cannot beat
cheap local full reuse in its tested regime.

The retained contributions are deliberately narrow:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item A block-aligned segmented-tail carrier and scheduler accounting that
separate requested, effective, and engine-realized actions.
\item A five-point real-online scaling observation with a 7.13\% geometric-mean
throughput gain and a negative-to-positive transition as concurrency increases.
\item A preregistered repeated real-online comparison establishing the separate
fixed-policy boundary, plus an explicit raw-versus-aggregate provenance limit.
\end{enumerate}
We do not claim a KV store, transfer system, admission/eviction policy,
multi-granularity index, arbitrary token-level materialization, or universal
speedup. A burst-arrival hypothesis remains outside this paper until its
separate gate passes.

\section{Design: Scope and Runtime Boundary}
At request arrival the controller can request full reuse, a segmented prefix,
or recomputation. The carrier supports native full reuse and recomputation. For
partial reuse, it caps lookup and shared-cache commit at an aligned block
boundary and isolates the recomputed tail in a request-scoped hash namespace.
Thus, partial reuse is block-aligned segmented reuse, not arbitrary token-level
materialization.

The runtime records three quantities. Requested is the policy output; effective
is the action after alignment and dominance checks; and realized is the engine
counter. It also records lookup, alignment, cache commit, tail isolation,
recomputed-tail tokens, and fallback reasons. A requested partial action is
therefore never reported as realized reuse.

\subsection{Physical break-even boundary}
Let $C_F$ be the cost of full reuse, $C_R$ the cost of recomputing the entire
prompt, and
\begin{equation}
C_P(b)=L_b+A_b+K_b+R_{\mathrm{tail}}(b)+I_b
\end{equation}
the cost of partial reuse at aligned boundary $b$: lookup, alignment, cache
commit, tail recomputation, and isolation. If the selector itself costs $D$,
then choosing action $a$ can beat full reuse only when
\begin{equation}
C_F-C_a > D.
\end{equation}
This inequality is the paper's mechanism test, not a fitted performance model.
The available headroom is regime dependent. In the Qwen2.5 M2 carrier, full
reuse avoids the recomputed tail while partial reuse still pays alignment and
commit costs; the offline ranking selects full reuse in all six main rounds,
matching the online winner. In the unified Qwen3.5 observation, the advantage
appears only as concurrency rises, but the protocol does not isolate which
runtime-path interaction supplies that advantage.

\section{Evidence Classes and Method}
We label every result by evidence class. Offline cost-model analysis uses
workload-derived inputs to inspect the three-action surface and oracle headroom;
it cannot demonstrate a serving gain. Real-online evidence comes from fresh
service lifecycles through the live path. Derived artifacts are tables and
verdicts rebuilt from those data. A model-selected partial action is never
promoted to realized reuse or online speedup.

The unified Qwen3.5 observation is pinned by the public Frontier evidence record
and its per-run manifest hashes in \texttt{vllm-hust-benchmark}. The retained M2
online bundles and generated reconstruction are in the benchmark evidence
collection pinned by \texttt{PAPER\_INPUTS.json}; the independent graph/eager
traces and custody manifest are under \url{docs/wavemat/}. These sources support
different protocols and are never pooled into one estimator.

The Qwen3.5 protocol uses Qwen3.5-35B-A3B in BF16 on two Ascend 910B2 chips,
TP2, FULL\_AND\_PIECEWISE graph mode, and a fixed SWE prefix-reuse workload.
Each concurrency point 1/2/4/8/16 is a 900-second real-online window. The
candidate is compared with the exact published Native series; no MOD-specific
Native rerun or matched repetition was performed. Prompt identity, token usage,
completion markers, output budgets, source revisions, clean release, and
controller/scheduler activity were checked for each point.

The primary online study uses Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, graph mode, block size 128,
two existing workloads, three temporally blocked rounds per workload, and a
fresh lifecycle for every controller/fixed-policy cell. It compares always
recompute, always full reuse, and the current controller. The lifecycle, not an
individual request, is the statistical unit. All 18/18 retained raw bundles
validate, containing 576/576 successful requests. Earlier single-run
descriptive matrices are not used as online performance evidence.

The controller was invoked 1,845 times: 1,745 full-reuse, 30 partial-reuse, and

exercised, but the positive observation cannot be attributed to partial reuse.

\section{Graph/Eager Diagnostic Boundary}
The separate graph/eager diagnostic tests an upstream layerwise KV path on a
TP=1 Ascend 910B2 with DeepSeek-V2-Lite. It uses paired eager and PIECEWISE
graph arms at static prefetch 1/2/4 and retains eight raw trace assets with
content checksums. Device-timeline overlap is low in both modes (0--4.62\%),
but no repeatable graph-only regression is observed; the p=2 gate trace
correlates all 52 eligible prefetch loads in each mode. Paired replay checks
are also clean. This is a Stop for a graph-specific mechanism, not an
end-to-end acceleration result and not evidence for a new overlap runtime.

\section{Limitations, Provenance, and Conclusion}
Of 141 completed controller lifecycles, 39 have in-repository/release raw bundles and
can be checked request-by-request: 18 main-matrix, 12 pilot, and 9 confirmation
lifecycles. The remaining 102 have per-run aggregates and verdicts only because
their raw bundles were not preserved; they are aggregate-only supporting
evidence. The graph/eager diagnostic separately retains 8/8 raw trace assets with checksums.
Generated tables are derived artifacts, not another evidence class.
The two compact tables consumed directly by this manuscript are checked in and
content-addressed in \texttt{PAPER\_INPUTS.json}, which also pins the benchmark
collection manifest and release archive used to restore them.

The positive observation is bounded to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, its pinned Ascend TP2
runtime, five single 900-second candidate windows, and the exact previously
published Native series. It has no MOD-specific matched repetitions and does
not isolate the gain among controller logic, APC/bypass outcomes, or runtime
interactions. The negative result is separately bounded to the tested
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct carrier, workload catalog, policy, and repeated protocol.
Neither protocol transfers its sign to the other. Neither turns block alignment
into token-level materialization; partial proposals established no independent
online benefit in either study.

The M2 verdict remains \texttt{stop\_mechanism\_direction}: that fixed-policy
search is closed without per-workload retuning or post-hoc workload search. It
does not erase the separate Qwen3.5 load-dependent observation. The combined
paper claim is conditional: the controller-enabled runtime scales positively
at C4--C16, while cheap local full reuse leaves no measured action-selection
headroom in M2. Matched replication of the positive series, venue formatting,
author approval, and a pinned PDF build remain before external submission.
